# Review a domain case

Independent language and domain review is the highest-priority gap. Current translations and labels are AI-authored drafts. Review is not assumed from a model's successful response.

## Language review

Identify the case ID, language variety and relevant locale. Compare the request with the intended task and policy:

- Is the latest target/value preserved, including corrections and negation?
- Does the text distinguish permission, a proposal, acknowledgment and a condition?
- Is the polite request natural while retaining its explicit approval?
- Are English technical labels and IDs understandable in context?
- Are dates, currency and the declared input format preserved without importing a different geography or rule?
- Is the request genuinely ambiguous? If so, explain the competing readings rather than forcing an intended label.

Language alone does not establish culture, nationality or communication style. Native-authored alternatives should describe the actual linguistic feature being tested, not a group stereotype. Human speech and text styles require separate evidence.

## Domain review

The five policies are fictional research rules. Check whether the workflow boundary is coherent: pending request versus active service, incident versus reporting date, expressed permission versus verified authority, and automatic execution versus specialist review. Do not treat the fixture as legal, clinical, credit or insurance guidance. Identify missing operational facts and unrealistic assumptions.

## Submit a review

Open a language-review issue with case IDs, original wording, proposed correction and the reason it changes or preserves meaning. State whether the proposed wording is original and whether AI assistance was used. Provide attribution only with the reviewer's consent. Do not upload private customer conversations, credentials or recordings.

Keep frozen experiment inputs and outputs intact. Accepted corrections should create a new versioned case set with review provenance and new experiment artifacts. They must not retroactively change published scores or be described as untouched evaluation data.
